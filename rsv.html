Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV)

RSV is a common respiratory virus. It often feels like a cold, and it can be serious for infants, older adults, and people with heart or lung disease or a weaker immune system.

This page is a short summary. Full guidance is on the New York State RSV page (opens in a new tab).
Orange County programs are on the county RSV page (opens in a new tab).

RSV guidance

Symptoms

Symptoms usually start 4 to 6 days after exposure and build over several days. A cough can last weeks. Some people have mild or no symptoms.

  • Runny nose
  • Cough
  • Sneezing
  • Fever
  • Wheezing
  • Decreased appetite

RSV symptoms (opens in a new tab).

Higher risk

Infants and very young children, especially those born premature, older adults, and people with heart disease, chronic lung disease, diabetes, or a weaker immune system are more likely to develop pneumonia or need hospital care.

Protect yourself

Get immunized if you are eligible. Wash hands, clean frequently touched surfaces, cover coughs and sneezes, and stay home while sick. Keep infants and older adults away from people with respiratory symptoms, and consider a mask around them.

Testing and care

A health care provider diagnoses RSV, often from symptoms. Lab tests are used mainly for severe illness. There is no routine antiviral like there is for flu. Rest, fluids, and fever or pain medicine can help. Do not give aspirin to children, and ask a provider before giving a child nonprescription cold medicine.
Diagnosis and care (opens in a new tab)

Vaccines and infant immunization

New York State recommends an RSV immunization for infants, for children 8 to 19 months in certain risk groups, for adults 50 to 74 with risk conditions, for all adults 75 and older who have not had an RSV vaccine, and for pregnant people in their first eligible pregnancy. Pregnant people may receive Abrysvo at 32 through 36 weeks of pregnancy during RSV season. Infants may receive an antibody product such as nirsevimab (Beyfortus) or clesrovimab (Enflonsia). Talk with a health care provider. Pharmacists in New York State may give RSV vaccine to eligible older adults and pregnant people.

When to get help right away

  • Trouble breathing or breathing much faster than usual
  • Bluish or gray skin
  • Not drinking well, or fewer wet diapers than usual
  • Not waking up or interacting normally

Contact a health care provider right away. If someone cannot breathe or will not wake up, call 911.
