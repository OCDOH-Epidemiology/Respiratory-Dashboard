What do Low, Moderate, and High mean?
Low
Fewer people are seeking medical care for respiratory symptoms. Stay cautious during peak season.
Moderate
People are seeking care for respiratory symptoms. Stay up to date on vaccines and practice good hygiene.
High
More people are seeking care. Extra precautions—masking indoors and staying home when sick—are recommended.

About this dashboard

The activity dial is a countywide summary of respiratory illness intensity. Charts below show how COVID-19, flu, and RSV contribute over time and acute respiratory ED visit volume, and how school absenteeism is changing.

Levels can rise or fall week to week. Use the dial for a quick read of current conditions, and the trend charts to see which pathogens are driving activity during the season.

Vaccination coverage shows New York State rates from the NYS COVID-19 and Influenza Vaccination Data dashboards. It is updated when the state posts new figures on its website, so it may not change every week.

This dashboard is intended for public awareness. Clinical decisions should follow current CDC and New York State guidance, facility protocols, and patient-specific risk factors.

Data may lag.

  • The current respiratory season is October 3, 2026 through May 7, 2027.
  • “Current season” filters Emergency Department Visits charts to that window (from the season start onward as weekly data are added). After May 7, 2027, that completed season remains in the data file and is shown under “All available data,” which includes every week present in the file.
  • Weekly points use week-ending dates. Point to a week on a chart for the week-ending date and values, or open “Show data table” under each chart for every week in the selected range. Press Escape to close a chart tooltip. Use the week-range sliders (arrow keys, Page Up/Page Down, Home, End) to narrow the weeks shown.
  • School absenteeism is the countywide percent of students absent among schools that were open that week. Closed schools (holidays, snow days, and similar) are excluded. Summer weeks and full-closure weeks may be blank.
  • Data are loaded from data/weekly.json. Stub values may appear until the weekly export is connected.

Actions you can take

  • Get vaccinated against respiratory illnesses. COVID-19 and flu vaccines are recommended for people 6 months and older. RSV vaccine is recommended for people at high risk—talk with your health care provider for details. Go to vaccines.gov (opens in a new tab) for a location near you.
  • Wash hands often with soap and water for 20 seconds, especially after coughing or sneezing.
  • Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow.
  • Stay home if you are sick with respiratory illness. Return to normal activity when symptoms have improved and you are fever-free for 24 hours.

  • Consider wearing a well-fitted mask when around others indoors.
  • Avoid crowded areas when possible.
  • Improve air quality by bringing in fresh air from outside, purifying air indoors, or gathering outdoors.
