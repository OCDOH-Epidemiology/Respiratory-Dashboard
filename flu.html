Influenza (Flu)

Flu is a respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. It spreads when a sick person coughs, sneezes, or is in close contact, and it can lead to serious complications.

This page is a short summary. Full guidance is on the New York State flu page (opens in a new tab).
Orange County programs are on the county flu page (opens in a new tab).

Flu guidance

Symptoms

Flu symptoms usually start suddenly, about 1 to 4 days after exposure. Some people, especially children, also have vomiting or diarrhea.

  • Fever or chills
  • Cough
  • Sore throat
  • Runny or stuffy nose
  • Muscle or body aches
  • Headache
  • Tiredness

Symptoms and how flu spreads (opens in a new tab).

Higher risk

Young children, older adults, pregnant people, and people with heart or lung disease, asthma, diabetes, or a weaker immune system are more likely to get very sick. Flu during pregnancy raises the risk of premature labor.
Who should get a flu vaccine (opens in a new tab)

Protect yourself

Get a flu vaccine every year. Avoid close contact with people who are sick, cover coughs and sneezes, wash your hands, and stay home until you have been fever-free for at least 24 hours without fever-reducing medicine and you are getting better.
Prevention (opens in a new tab)

Testing and treatment

Flu and COVID-19 can look alike, so testing may be needed. Antiviral drugs work best when started soon after symptoms begin. They are not a substitute for vaccination. Rest and fluids help, and over-the-counter medicine may ease symptoms.

Vaccines

Everyone 6 months and older should get a flu vaccine every year. The vaccine cannot cause the flu. It is offered as a shot or, for some people, a nasal spray, at doctor’s offices, clinics, and pharmacies. Most people should get it in September or October. Protection takes about two weeks.
Flu vaccine (opens in a new tab)

When to call a health care provider

  • Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
  • Pain or pressure in the chest or abdomen
  • Sudden dizziness
  • Confusion
  • Severe or persistent vomiting
  • Symptoms improve, then fever and a worse cough return

If someone cannot breathe or will not wake up, call 911.
